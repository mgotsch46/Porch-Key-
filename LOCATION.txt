LOCATION SHARING — what gets recorded, and what it is good for
==============================================================

Short version: nothing is recorded unless the buyer switches it on, it records a
position only when they open the app, and switching it off deletes the history.


HOW IT WORKS
------------
1. The buyer is asked once, at sign-in, on a screen that explains in plain words
   what is collected and why. Nothing is recorded before they say yes.
2. Saying yes then triggers the phone's own permission prompt. Both have to be
   granted. Neither you nor the app can grant them on their behalf.
3. From then on, each time they open the app it records one position: latitude,
   longitude, accuracy, and the timestamp.
4. They can switch it off any time under Settings. Doing so deletes every position
   already recorded — not hides, deletes.


WHAT IT IS NOT
--------------
It is NOT continuous tracking. There is no background tracking, no geofence, no
"where are they right now" lookup. If the buyer does not open the app for three
weeks, there is a three-week hole in the record. That is by design: continuous
background location would need a native app, App Store review of a persistent
location entitlement, and a much harder justification under state privacy law.


WHAT YOU SEE
------------
On the loan page there is a card, "Where the buyer's phone has been":

  - a status chip: at the property / a mile or two away / further out
  - when the last position was recorded, and how long ago
  - a link opening it in Google Maps
  - the full list of recorded positions, newest first, each with the distance from
    the property, the GPS accuracy, and its own map link

Distance is straight-line from the property's own coordinates, which get saved
when you pick the address from the suggestion list. If a property was entered by
typing the address without picking a suggestion, it has no coordinates and the
distance column shows a dash — re-pick the address to fix it.


READING IT HONESTLY
-------------------
Phone GPS is good to roughly 30–100 feet outdoors and considerably worse indoors,
so anything under a quarter mile counts as "at the home". The accuracy column is
worth a glance: a fix reported as accurate to 3,000 feet came off a cell tower,
not a satellite, and should not be leaned on.

A pattern of positions consistently far from the property is worth a conversation.
It is not proof of anything on its own, and it is not a substitute for a drive-by
or a written occupancy provision in your agreement.


THE LEGAL SIDE — worth reading before you rely on this
------------------------------------------------------
Precise geolocation is "sensitive personal information" under the California,
Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia and several other state privacy acts. Practically,
that means:

  - consent has to be freely given. It cannot be a condition of using the app or
    of servicing the loan. The app is built this way; keep it that way in how you
    talk about it with buyers.
  - the stated purpose has to match the actual use. The consent screen says it is
    for confirming occupancy and helping locate the nearest cash-payment retailer.
    Using it for something else undercuts the consent.
  - buyers can ask for their data and ask for it deleted. Both are built in.

Have your attorney look at the consent wording and confirm it matches your
agreement's occupancy provisions before you rely on this in any dispute.
